FICTIONAL CASE
The Misrouted Train Incident

Station staff receive a platform-change notice, but no one is assigned to update signage or passenger announcements. Riders follow outdated directions and gather at the wrong platform, causing confusion, crowding, and delays. This is a fictional communication case, not an operating procedure or a report of a real incident.

↗
What to doBreakout groups of 3–5 · 18 min total · 10 min breakout
  1. Read the fictional case together.
  2. Discuss the four questions below and record brief responses.
  3. Choose one spokesperson to report one communication failure and one consequence.
  4. Return for the instructor-led debrief.

Takeaway: Connect each communication failure with a possible operational or passenger effect.

SAME FACTS, DIFFERENT MESSAGE
A new work-order reporting process begins Monday

All new requests must use a new online form. Mobile access is unavailable during the first week, so field employees must use a designated workstation. Existing open requests remain in the current system. The change is intended to reduce duplicate requests, but the transition may slow processing at first.

↗
What to doGroups of 3–4 · 22 min total · 10 min drafting

  2. Draft a different message for the operations manager, experienced field employee, and new employee.

  4. Choose a spokesperson and explain one intentional difference during the report-out.

Listening & next steps / Paired practice · Slide 41

Listening to Understand

Follow the instructor’s directions. Save your own thinking as you work.

10
Paired practice · Slide 41Local autosave
1Focus

Give full attention. Stop planning your reply.

2Signal

Show engagement in a way that fits the person and setting.

3Reflect

Paraphrase the point. Acknowledge relevant emotion without guessing motives.

4Confirm

Ask a respectful question or restate the agreed action.

↗
What to doPairs · two rounds · 22 min · 2 min setup, 5 + 5 min practice, 8 min debrief
  1. Choose a low-risk workplace frustration or one of the fictional prompts below.
  2. In Round 1, Partner A speaks while Partner B listens and confirms understanding before offering solutions.
  3. Switch roles for Round 2 when directed.
  4. Capture one paraphrase, one clarifying question, and one understanding check.
  5. Debrief which of the four listening moves changed the interaction.
Prepared fictional scenarios (select to expand)
  • Priorities changed three times this week. You are unclear what to finish first.
  • A handoff was missing information, and you spent extra time reconstructing the work.
  • A schedule change affected your work, but you learned about it late.
