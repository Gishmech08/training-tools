Slide 5 · 10 minLocal autosave

Instructor preparation & facilitation

Surface multiple possible interpretations before teaching the hidden-filter model.

Slide 5 · 10 minFacilitator view

SETUP

Before you begin

  • Display the five-panel workplace comic, not the next slide with the four hidden-filter labels.
  • Keep chat or verbal discussion available. Do not reveal the explanation before participants speak.

DELIVERY

Lead the activity

  1. Give 1 minute to read the comic silently.
  2. Ask where the first unclear instruction appeared; take two or three initial responses.
  3. Ask about supervisor, employee, and coworker assumptions at each handoff.
  4. Invite one revised team message that includes the exact 8:30 a.m. start, arrival time, room, and confirmation.

DISCUSSION

Ask / debrief

  • Where did communication first break down?
  • Who could have asked a clarification question?
  • How would you rewrite the team message?

EXPECTED THEMES

Listen for

  • “Early” is undefined.
  • Original briefing time is assumed rather than confirmed.
  • No explicit acknowledgement or read-back.
TRANSITION

Misrouted Train Incident

Facilitation steps, expected themes, and class-level eWhiteboard.

03
Slide 10 · 18 minLocal autosave

Instructor preparation & facilitation

Connect a fictional station information handoff to operational, safety, and rider experience.

Slide 10 · 18 minFacilitator view

SETUP

Before you begin

  • State that the case is fictional. Do not attribute the scenario to an actual WMATA incident.
  • Prepare breakout groups of three to five and a 10-minute timer.
  • Give each group a spokesperson and remind participants to record their own responses.

DELIVERY

Lead the activity

  1. Read the short platform-change scenario.
  2. Send groups into breakouts for 10 minutes and give a two-minute warning near the end.
  3. Use the remaining eight minutes for brief report-out. Capture one failure and one consequence per group.
  4. Debrief safety/crowding, customer experience, operational delays/rework, and confidence in information.

DISCUSSION

Ask / debrief

  • Where was message ownership unclear?
  • What happened when announcements and signs did not match the new platform?
  • What assignment or verification step might prevent recurrence?

EXPECTED THEMES

Listen for

  • No clear owner for signs and announcements.
  • Conflicting information reaching passengers.
  • Confusion, crowding, delays, frustration, and diminished trust.

06
Slide 25 · 18 minLocal autosave

Instructor preparation & facilitation

Find what each style contributes, where it can become difficult, and what others should understand.

Slide 25 · 18 minFacilitator view

SETUP

Before you begin

  • Assign rooms based on each person’s individual Style Squad result.
  • Two participants are enough for a discussion. For a single participant, join if coverage allows or combine singleton rooms without changing results.
  • Prepare an eight-minute breakout and a two-minute warning at minute six.

DELIVERY

Lead the activity

  1. Groups answer the three questions and nominate a spokesperson.
  2. During report-out, capture one strength, challenge, and need per style.
  3. Avoid fixed labels or judgments; make style adjustments the practical result.

DISCUSSION

Ask / debrief

  • What works well with your style?
  • When does that same tendency cause difficulty?
  • What do you wish teammates understood?

EXPECTED THEMES

Listen for

  • Dominance: clarity and pace.
  • Influence: energy and discussion.
  • Steadiness: stability and impact on people.
  • Conscientiousness: accuracy and preparation.

Instructor preparation & facilitation

Adapt one work-order process change for three audiences without changing any underlying fact.

Slide 29 · 22 minFacilitator view

SETUP

Before you begin

  • Create groups of three or four. Assign a spokesperson and ten minutes of drafting time.

DELIVERY

Lead the activity

Challenging Conversation Lab

Facilitation steps, expected themes, and class-level eWhiteboard.

08
Slide 32 · 10 minLocal autosave

Instructor preparation & facilitation

Facilitate the three live scenarios, then compare response tradeoffs rather than scoring answers.

Slide 32 · 10 minFacilitator view
↗ Launch the live Challenging Conversation Lab opens a new tab

SETUP

Before you begin

  • Open the linked HTML page in a browser window and screen-share that window.
  • The image on Slide 32 is the launch screen, not the activity itself. Select the picture to launch or use the URL.
  • Prepare chat or verbal response collection. No learner login or instructor-to-participant data connection is required.

DELIVERY

Lead the activity

  1. Allow about 1 minute to launch and explain the format.
  2. For each scenario, read the situation and ask what learners would say BEFORE revealing anything.
  3. Select “Reveal response options,” examine 2–3 alternatives and their consequences, then select “Reveal debrief.”
  4. Select “Next scenario.” Allow about 2½ minutes per scenario and finish with a brief final comparison.
  5. Use the three scenario summary prompts below only as a fallback if the live HTML cannot load.

DISCUSSION

Ask / debrief

  • Scapegoating: How do you acknowledge the concern without joining the blame?
  • Put-down: How do you set a respectful boundary and identify the two missing log entries?
  • Stonewalling: How do you get the necessary handoff information without forcing a confrontation?

EXPECTED THEMES

Listen for

  • A factual next step, not personal attack.
  • Acknowledge frustration while maintaining accountability.
  • Set boundaries calmly.
  • Offer timing or follow-up when discussion stalls.

Listening & next steps / Paired practice · Slide 41

Listening to Understand

Facilitation steps, expected themes, and class-level eWhiteboard.

10
Slide 41 · 22 minLocal autosave

Instructor preparation & facilitation

Practice Focus, Signal, Reflect and Confirm in two low-risk listening rounds.

Slide 41 · 22 minFacilitator view

SETUP

Before you begin

  • Use paired breakouts and a visible timer.
  • Tell participants not to share sensitive details. Provide three prepared fictional scenarios when needed.
  • Allow about two minutes for instructions, two five-minute rounds and eight minutes for debrief.

DELIVERY

Lead the activity

  1. Round 1: Partner A speaks, Partner B listens. Listener does not jump straight to solving.
  2. At five minutes, switch roles.
  3. Ask listeners to paraphrase, acknowledge a relevant concern, ask a clarifying question and confirm understanding.
  4. Debrief with one paraphrase and one useful confirmation question from the room.

DISCUSSION

Ask / debrief

  • What was easiest?
  • What kept you from jumping to a solution?
  • How did you verify that you understood?

EXPECTED THEMES

Listen for

  • Undivided attention and suitable nonverbal signals.
  • Paraphrase in listener’s words.
  • No motive attribution.
  • An open question followed by a specific understanding check.
TRANSITION
Move to the next step
