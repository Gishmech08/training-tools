Day One

People, purpose, environment, ownership and breaking the work down.

DAY 1 · SLIDE 10 · Full group

Electronic Workbook Orientation

Make sure every learner can open the participant workbook and understands how the group will use it across both days.

4 min
Participant workbook: Orientation / participant names

Instructor flow

  1. Post the participant workbook link in chat.
  2. Share your instructor workbook briefly and point out where participant names, activity sections, AI Assist and download controls appear.
  3. Confirm every group can open the workbook before moving on.
  4. Tell learners to keep the same browser and device when possible and to download a backup at the end of Day One.

Watch for

  • Access problems. Fix them now, before the first breakout.
  • Learners opening scenario updates early.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Open the participant workbook from the link in chat.
  2. Enter the names of everyone in your group.
  3. Keep this workbook open throughout the course.
  4. Your group will type directly into it during activities.
  5. Save a backup at the end of Day One and a final copy at the end of Day Two.
DAY 1 · SLIDE 17 · Breakout rooms, 3 to 4

Build the Meridian Move Team

Build the project role list, map stakeholders and create two engagement plans.

30 min
Participant workbook: Meridian Move scenario brief + Stakeholder Grid

Instructor flow

  1. Keep the same groups for both days.
  2. Assign one scribe per group.
  3. Give groups 20 minutes in rooms, then about 7 minutes to report out.
  4. Require groups to build their own stakeholder list before using AI.
  5. Ask each group to return ready to name one stakeholder they almost missed.

Watch for

  • Roles with no owner.
  • Groups treating the grid as a formula instead of a judgment call.
  • AI suggestions copied without checking the scenario.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Identify the roles the Meridian Move needs and who plays each one.
  2. Place each stakeholder on the influence/interest grid.
  3. Draft a one-paragraph engagement plan for your two most important stakeholders.
  4. If you use AI, build your own list first, then use it only to check for people you may have missed.
  5. Be ready to name one stakeholder your group almost missed.
DAY 1 · SLIDE 24 · Breakout rooms, 3 to 4

Write the Scope Statement

Produce a scope statement that two reasonable people would interpret the same way.

30 min
Participant workbook: Charter + Scope

Instructor flow

  1. Direct groups to the Charter + Scope section before opening rooms.
  2. Give 20 minutes in rooms: about 10 to draft, 5 to pressure-test and 5 to revise.
  3. Tell groups to preserve the original draft so the before-and-after change is visible.
  4. AI comes after the human draft and serves only as a pressure-test.

Watch for

  • No out-of-scope line.
  • Success criteria that describe activity instead of a measurable condition.
  • AI replacing the first human draft.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Draft the Meridian Move scope statement as a group.
  2. Include what is in scope, what is out of scope and one measurable success criterion per deliverable.
  3. Pressure-test the draft: where could two reasonable people interpret it differently?
  4. Revise the draft and mark the line your group debated most.
  5. If using AI, ask it to identify vague phrases. Your group decides what changes.
DAY 1 · SLIDE 28–29 · Scenario update + breakout rooms

SWOT the Meridian Move

Reassess the project after the budget cut and identify the single factor that changes the plan most.

22 min
Participant workbook: SWOT Grid
Unlock word: BUDGET

Instructor flow

  1. Deliver the budget cut as a live project update.
  2. Post the unlock word BUDGET in chat.
  3. Give groups 12 minutes to fill the four quadrants, then select one item that changes the plan most.
  4. Ask groups to use AI only after their grid is already populated.

Watch for

  • Internal issues placed in the external quadrant.
  • Groups solving the budget cut instead of analyzing the environment first.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Open the SWOT Grid using the unlock word from chat.
  2. Complete all four quadrants for the Meridian Move as it stands now, budget cut included.
  3. Add at least three entries per quadrant.
  4. Star the one item that most changes how you would run the project.
  5. Choose one insight to bring back to the full group.
DAY 1 · SLIDE 37 · Breakout rooms, pairs

Run the Check-In

Let learners experience the difference between support and interrogation.

12 min
Participant workbook: Check-In Practice

Instructor flow

  1. Create rooms of two. With an odd number, use one room of three with an observer.
  2. Give four minutes for round one, switch roles, then four minutes for round two.
  3. Broadcast the switch at four minutes and a one-minute warning near the end.
  4. Have each pair record one blocker and one next-step date in the workbook.

Watch for

  • Why are you behind? Redirect to What is in the way?
  • Pairs trying to solve the whole problem during the check-in.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. One person is the project lead. One person owns a task that is two days late.
  2. Ask only three questions: What moved? What is blocking you? What do you need from me?
  3. After four minutes, switch roles and run it again.
  4. Record the blocker you agreed to clear and the next-step date.
  5. Compare: which version felt like help and which felt like an audit?
DAY 1 · SLIDE 39 then 38 · Individual, main room

Message Devon, Then Debrief Tone

Practice an accountability message that protects both the deadline and the relationship.

20 min
Participant workbook: Devon Message
Unlock word: DEVON

Instructor flow

  1. Present Slide 39 before the Tone Matters debrief.
  2. Post DEVON in chat.
  3. Give learners 5 quiet minutes to write individually.
  4. After writing, return to the Tone Matters debrief and invite two or three examples.

Watch for

  • Accusatory openings.
  • Messages so soft they never name the missed deliverable.
  • AI wording that does not sound like the learner.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Open the Devon scenario update with the unlock word from chat.
  2. Write the message you would send Devon.
  3. Name the missed deliverable and its impact.
  4. Ask what is happening before making assumptions.
  5. Set a clear next step or time to reconnect.
  6. If you use AI, rewrite the draft in your own voice.
DAY 1 · SLIDE 43 · Breakout rooms, 3 to 4

Break Down One Deliverable

Build one WBS branch to Level 3 with one owner per task.

32 min
Participant workbook: WBS

Instructor flow

  1. Have groups claim IT cutover, physical move or people and communications.
  2. Give 20 minutes in rooms and 10 minutes for report-out.
  3. Require owners to come from the role list the group created earlier.
  4. Use AI only as a check against missing standard sub-tasks.

Watch for

  • Tasks with two owners or no owner.
  • Level 2 items written as activities instead of deliverables.
  • Tasks that are too large to complete in about a week.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Choose one branch: IT cutover, physical move or people and communications.
  2. List the deliverables in that branch, nouns first.
  3. Split each deliverable until every task has one owner and fits in about a week.
  4. Assign one real role from your team map to every task.
  5. If using AI, cut anything that falls outside your scope.

Day Two

Estimating, scheduling, risk, staying on track, capstone, closeout and transfer to real work.

DAY 2 · SLIDE 46 · Full group

Day Two Reconnect

Reactivate Day One outputs and make sure every group has the work it needs for Day Two.

15 min
Participant workbook: Day Two Reconnect

Instructor flow

  1. Ask every group to open the same participant workbook used on Day One or restore its saved backup.
  2. Confirm they can see their stakeholder grid, charter and scope, SWOT and WBS branch.
  3. Keep the same groups and rooms.
  4. Do not reteach Day One. Use one brief reconnect question, then move.

Watch for

  • Missing Day One work. Pair the group with a working copy so the day keeps moving.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Open your Day One workbook or restore your saved backup.
  2. Confirm your group can see four Day One outputs: team and stakeholders, charter and scope, SWOT and WBS branch.
  3. Keep the workbook open. You will reuse these sections throughout Day Two.
DAY 2 · SLIDE 50 · Breakout rooms, pairs

Three-Point Estimate

Expose uncertainty in task estimates before building the schedule.

12 min
Participant workbook: Three-Point Estimate

Instructor flow

  1. Pair learners within their existing group so estimates stay connected to the same workbook.
  2. Give pairs 6 minutes in rooms.
  3. Have them estimate three tasks using best, most likely and worst case.
  4. Use the widest spread as the bridge to risk.

Watch for

  • The same number entered three times.
  • Learners estimating work they do not understand without naming assumptions.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Pick three tasks from your group's WBS branch.
  2. For each task, record a best case, most likely case and worst case.
  3. Compare the spread between the three estimates.
  4. Identify the task with the widest spread and be ready to explain why.
DAY 2 · SLIDE 53 · Breakout rooms, 3 to 4

Build the Schedule

Turn the group's WBS and estimates into a defensible schedule.

26 min
Participant workbook: WBS + estimates + timeline

Instructor flow

  1. Confirm a scribe before opening rooms.
  2. Give groups 18 minutes in rooms and a two-minute warning.
  3. Require a marked longest chain, a committed date and one condition that could break it.
  4. Treat AI as a dependency check, not the source of the schedule.

Watch for

  • A date chosen first and justified later.
  • Everything sequenced when some work can run in parallel.
  • Everything put in parallel with no dependencies.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Take the WBS branch and estimates you already built.
  2. Put the tasks in order and mark which work can run in parallel.
  3. Place the tasks on the 12-week timeline.
  4. Trace the longest chain of dependent work.
  5. Name the date your group would commit to and one condition that could break it.
DAY 2 · SLIDE 59–60 · Scenario update + breakout rooms

Hunt the Risks

Prioritize current project risks and choose responses with named owners.

23 min
Participant workbook: Risk Matrix
Unlock word: DELAY

Instructor flow

  1. Deliver the vendor delay and network slip as a live update.
  2. Post DELAY in chat.
  3. Give groups 14 minutes in rooms: find and place risks, select the top three, choose responses and owners.
  4. Require a concrete owner or next action for every top risk.

Watch for

  • Issues that already happened listed as future risks.
  • Response plans with no owner.
  • Generic AI risks kept even when they do not fit Meridian.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Open the new scenario update using the unlock word from chat.
  2. Brainstorm risks and place each on the likelihood/impact grid.
  3. Select the top three risks that deserve active attention.
  4. Choose a response for each: mitigate, avoid, transfer, accept or escalate.
  5. Name who acts for each top risk.
DAY 2 · SLIDE 64 · Full group

Keeping Score Reference

Give learners three lightweight tracking tools they can reuse.

4 min
Participant workbook: Tracking Notes

Instructor flow

  1. Walk through the milestone tracker, issue log and decision log.
  2. Explain that these are reference tools in the workbook. No breakout is needed.
  3. Ask which one would help most on learners' current projects.

Watch for

  • Turning the reference into a new reporting burden.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Milestone tracker: planned date, current forecast date and one owner.
  2. Issue log: what already happened, who is fixing it and by when.
  3. Decision log: what was decided, when and why.
  4. Keep each tool simple enough that the team will update it.
DAY 2 · SLIDE 66 · Breakout rooms, 3 to 4

Raise the Change

Convert the schedule shock into a decision request the COO can act on.

11 min
Participant workbook: Change Request

Instructor flow

  1. Use the same breakout groups.
  2. Have groups draft three lines in the workbook, then post them in chat for cross-read.
  3. Require the decision needed from the COO and the date it is needed by.

Watch for

  • Groups describing the problem well but never asking for a decision.
  • Vague impact language such as it will be tight.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Write three lines: what changed, what it costs and what you recommend.
  2. State the decision you need from the COO.
  3. State the date you need that decision by.
  4. Post your draft in chat and read another group's draft.
  5. Ask: would you approve theirs? What is missing?
DAY 2 · SLIDE 69 · Individual + direct-message pair swap

Write the COO Update

Practice concise sponsor communication tied to project status and decisions.

7 min
Participant workbook: Communication Plan + COO Update

Instructor flow

  1. This is not a breakout activity.
  2. Give learners time to write a five-line update individually.
  3. Pair learners by name or participant list order for a direct-message edit.
  4. Ask them to delete every line the COO does not need and return the tighter version.

Watch for

  • Status detail with no decision or request.
  • Updates longer than five lines after editing.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Write the COO's milestone update in five lines or fewer.
  2. Cover: where we are, what changed, what you are doing about it, what you need from the COO and when the next update will come.
  3. Send your draft by direct chat message to your assigned partner.
  4. Edit your partner's draft by removing anything the COO does not need, then send it back.
DAY 2 · SLIDE 71 · Breakout rooms, 3 to 4

Capstone: Two Weeks Less

Use the full toolkit together after a final schedule compression.

32 min
Participant workbook: Capstone
Unlock word: TENWEEKS

Instructor flow

  1. Post TENWEEKS in chat.
  2. Have groups keep their schedule, risk matrix and stakeholder grid open alongside the capstone section.
  3. Give 22 minutes in rooms: about 8 to re-plan milestones, 5 to update risks and stakeholders, 6 to write the COO message and 3 to rehearse.
  4. Require one presenter per group.

Watch for

  • Compressing every task by the same percentage instead of making trade-offs.
  • Cutting scope without naming who approves it.
  • COO messages longer than three sentences.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. The move now finishes in 10 weeks, not 12. Everything else still applies.
  2. Re-plan the milestones. Decide what moves, compresses or leaves scope.
  3. Update your top three risks.
  4. Identify the stakeholders who need engagement now.
  5. Write a three-sentence message to the COO: new plan, trade-offs and what you need.
  6. Choose one person to present the three sentences.
DAY 2 · SLIDE 79 · Breakout rooms, 3 to 4

Run the Lessons Learned

Run a blameless review and capture one useful lesson with a home.

18 min
Participant workbook: Closeout Checklist + lessons learned

Instructor flow

  1. Keep the same groups.
  2. Give 13 minutes in rooms and a two-minute warning.
  3. Tell groups to stop the first blame statement and redirect to process, decision or condition.
  4. Take one lesson per group when rooms close.

Watch for

  • Comments focused on people instead of process.
  • Lessons too vague to act on.
  • A lesson stored somewhere future teams will not check.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Hold the meeting, do not discuss how one might work.
  2. Ask three questions: What worked? What did not work? What should the next project lead know?
  3. One person captures the discussion.
  4. No names, no defending and no re-litigating the vendor issue.
  5. Agree on one lesson worth recording and where a future project lead would find it.
DAY 2 · SLIDE 80 · Individual, main room

Your Project, Your Plan

Produce one planning artifact each learner can use on a real project.

22 min
Participant workbook: One-Page Plan

Instructor flow

  1. Keep the main room quiet. No breakout rooms.
  2. Give 15 minutes of individual work, then 5 minutes to post the least-certain box in chat and tighten entries.
  3. Help learners narrow projects that are too broad.
  4. Remind learners to download or save this page before the activity ends.

Watch for

  • Projects too broad for the available time.
  • No measurable success criterion.
  • Tasks without owners.
  • Risks written as issues that already happened.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Pick one real project you currently lead, coordinate or contribute to.
  2. Complete the one-page plan: purpose, one measurable success criterion, five tasks with owners, top three risks and first check-in date.
  3. Mark the box you are least sure about.
  4. Post that item in chat for the debrief.
  5. Save or download your completed plan before you move on.
DAY 2 · SLIDE 82 · Individual, main room

Your First Move

Close the loop from the Day One challenge list with one specific, dated commitment.

9 min
Participant workbook: Action Plan

Instructor flow

  1. Reopen the Day One challenge whiteboard and share it briefly.
  2. Direct learners to the Action Plan section.
  3. Give 6 minutes of individual work.
  4. Tell learners to set the two-week self-check on their calendar now.
  5. Remind everyone to download the final workbook before leaving.

Watch for

  • I will use all of it. Push for one tool.
  • No named project.
  • No date for the self-check.

Learner-facing instructions

  1. Choose one tool from this course and one real project where you will use it this week.
  2. Set a two-week self-check: did you use it, what happened and what got in the way?
  3. Name one colleague who will know about your commitment.
  4. Save or download your final workbook before closing the browser.
