Before You Begin · Full group

00 · Using Your eWorkbook

Get every participant into the eWorkbook and confident about saving their work before class begins.

2 min
Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Start Here

Instructor flow

  1. Post the participant eWorkbook link in chat and ask everyone to open it.
  2. Give participants a moment to confirm the workbook loads.
  3. Point out the left navigation, activity fields and Download / Save My Work controls.
  4. Resolve access problems now rather than during the first activity.

Watch for

  • A participant whose workbook will not load. Pair them with a colleague for the session.
  • Participants closing the workbook or treating browser storage as a permanent backup.

Debrief / listen for

  • No formal debrief required.

Transition

Now that everyone has the workbook open, let’s begin.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Open the participant eWorkbook from the link in chat in a separate browser tab.
  2. Keep the eWorkbook open throughout the session.
  3. Use the left navigation to move to each activity when directed.
  4. Before closing the workbook, use Download / Save My Work so you keep a copy of your entries.
Opening and Framing · Individual, main room

01 · Start with Your Challenge

Capture each participant’s own time-management challenge so the day has something concrete to connect to and return to later.

3 min
Go to Whiteboard Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Start with Your Challenge

Instructor flow

  1. Frame this as a quick needs check, not a problem-solving discussion.
  2. Give about 1 minute for individual entry.
  3. Invite two or three responses in chat or aloud.
  4. Note recurring themes you can reference later.

Watch for

  • Keep the discussion moving. The point is to capture themes, not solve them yet.

Debrief / listen for

  • What tends to create the most pressure for you?

Transition

You will hear several of those challenges reflected in what we are covering today.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Open Start with Your Challenge in the eWorkbook.
  2. Take about 1 minute to enter one time-management challenge you would like to handle better.
  3. Leave the response in the workbook. You will return to it during the Action Plan.
Section 1 · Take Control of Multiple Tasks · Individual, then pairs

02 · Your Top Time Drains

Move participants from naming frustrations to identifying where action is possible.

10 min
Go to Whiteboard Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Your Top Time Drains

Instructor flow

  1. Give 2 minutes to enter three time drains.
  2. Give 2 minutes to classify them and note reasoning.
  3. Open pairs for about 3 minutes to compare classifications.
  4. Return to the main room and debrief the reasoning.

Watch for

  • Participants who classify everything as Adapt. Ask what would have to be true for one item to move to Influence.
  • Validate real operational constraints rather than forcing a different classification.

Debrief / listen for

  • Which drain generated the most disagreement about Control, Influence or Adapt?
  • What changed when you focused on where action is possible?

Transition

Now let’s look at the work itself and decide what belongs where.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Enter your top three time drains.
  2. Classify each as Control, Influence or Adapt and note why.
  3. Compare your classifications with a partner.
  4. Discuss any item you viewed differently.
Section 1 · Take Control of Multiple Tasks · Groups of 3–5

03 · Clean Up the Task Pile

Practice sorting work before using AI or another tool to organize it.

10 min
Go to Whiteboard Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Clean Up the Task Pile

Instructor flow

  1. Brief the categories before opening rooms.
  2. Keep the sequence intact: participants decide first, then compare with AI or another tool.
  3. Give groups 5 minutes to sort the eight tasks.
  4. Debrief the ambiguous items and what context a tool would be missing.

Watch for

  • Groups that sort quickly without discussing ambiguous items.
  • If AI is unavailable, compare verbally with how a context-free tool might classify two items.

Debrief / listen for

  • Which item created the most disagreement?
  • What information would an AI tool need before its answer deserved your trust?

Transition

Once work is sorted, the next challenge is deciding what can realistically fit.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. In your breakout room, take 5 minutes to sort all eight tasks.
  2. Use the categories Project, Next Action, Today, Waiting or Remove.
  3. Capture your group’s decisions in the eWorkbook.
  4. Be ready to explain the reasoning behind at least one ambiguous item.
Section 2 · Manage Time with Intention · Individual or pairs

04 · Design a Workable Day

Build a realistic workday that protects priorities and leaves usable capacity.

12 min
Go to Whiteboard Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Design a Workable Day

Instructor flow

  1. Decide before briefing whether participants work alone or in pairs.
  2. Give about 8 minutes to build the day.
  3. Prompt participants to make deliberate choices about what is left out.
  4. Debrief what they protected and where they created buffer.

Watch for

  • Schedules with every minute filled. Ask what happens when something breaks at 10:15.

Debrief / listen for

  • What did you protect even though something else had to wait?
  • Where did you leave buffer, and why?
  • What did you deliberately leave out?

Transition

A workable day depends on priorities. Next we will test those priorities when capacity gets tight.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Build an 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. plan in the eWorkbook.
  2. Place fixed commitments first.
  3. Protect the highest-priority work.
  4. Add routine work only where it fits.
  5. Leave buffer. The goal is not to fit everything.
Section 3 · Manage Multiple Priorities · Groups of 3–5

05 · Re-Prioritize a Crowded Day

Practice ranking work when available capacity is smaller than the task list.

15 min
Go to Whiteboard Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Re-Prioritize a Crowded Day

Instructor flow

  1. Give groups about 9 minutes.
  2. Visit rooms and challenge the reasoning, not the ranking.
  3. Return to the main room and compare what groups moved, shrank or dropped.

Watch for

  • Groups that rank purely by stated deadline. Ask what they did with the valuable item nobody gave a deadline.

Debrief / listen for

  • What did you move or drop?
  • Which decision was hardest to defend?
  • What mattered besides deadline?

Transition

Priority becomes clearer when we separate urgency from importance.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Working in breakout rooms, review all eight tasks and their estimated times.
  2. Rank the tasks using value, sequence, consequence and dependency, not deadline alone.
  3. Decide what fits in the four available hours.
  4. Identify what must Move, Shrink or Go.
  5. Capture the group’s tradeoffs in the eWorkbook.
Section 4 · Urgent Compared with Important · Pairs or groups

06 · Matrix Practice

Apply urgent/important logic while recognizing that missing context changes classification.

12 min
Go to Whiteboard Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Matrix Practice

Instructor flow

  1. Give groups about 7 minutes.
  2. During debrief, choose two or three items where groups disagreed.
  3. Ask what information or role perspective caused the difference.

Watch for

  • Do not force one universal classification where the role or context changes the answer.

Debrief / listen for

  • Where did you put the unresolved equipment issue? What drove your decision?
  • Which item needed more context before you could classify it confidently?

Transition

Even a good priority system gets harder to use when pressure starts changing how we think.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Place each task in the urgent/important matrix.
  2. For each task, identify the next action.
  3. Capture your group’s responses in the eWorkbook.
  4. If you need more information to classify an item, say what you need.
Section 5 · Coping with Pressure and Stress · Individual, main room

07 · Recognize Overload Early

Help participants identify the personal signal that tells them overload is starting.

1–2 min
Go to Whiteboard Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Recognize Overload Early

Instructor flow

  1. Give about 1 minute for private reflection.
  2. Invite a few examples, focusing on signals participants can notice early.

Watch for

  • Keep this practical. The goal is an observable early signal plus a response, not a long discussion about stress.

Debrief / listen for

  • What signal shows up first for you?
  • What could you do at that point, before overload gets worse?

Transition

Now we will apply that idea when the day itself changes.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Select the overload signal that tends to show up first for you.
  2. Enter one response you can use when you notice that signal.
  3. Be ready to share if you choose.
Section 5 · Coping with Pressure and Stress · Groups of 3–5

08 · Pressure Reset: Your Day Changes at 1:30 p.m.

Apply a deliberate reset to a day that cannot be rescued by simply working harder.

12 min
Go to Whiteboard Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Pressure Reset

Instructor flow

  1. Say plainly that this scenario cannot be solved by squeezing everything harder.
  2. Give teams about 7 minutes.
  3. Debrief for about 4 minutes using the same four decision points.

Watch for

  • Groups that keep every original commitment and add the new request.
  • Groups that decide everything but communicate with nobody.

Debrief / listen for

  • What did you protect?
  • What moved?
  • Who needed communication?
  • What was your first action when you restarted?
  • Which decision felt hardest, and why?

Transition

Managing pressure sometimes requires a difficult conversation about what cannot fit.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Working in breakout rooms for about 7 minutes, review the 1:30 p.m. changes.
  2. Decide what you will protect.
  3. Decide what moves or stops.
  4. Identify who needs communication.
  5. Choose the first action you will take when you restart.
  6. Capture the decisions in the eWorkbook.
Section 6 · Manage Difficult Situations · Individual practice

09 · Say “No” Without Creating a Dead End

Practice declining as a negotiation rather than a refusal.

7 min
Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Say “No” Without Creating a Dead End

Instructor flow

  1. Teach the four-step response before the writing activity.
  2. Give about 1 minute for participants to rewrite the response in their own language.
  3. Invite two or three volunteers.
  4. Coach toward concise, neutral wording that includes an alternative and confirmation.

Watch for

  • Do not let a version pass that only states the constraint. Every response needs a path forward.

Debrief / listen for

  • What makes this different from simply saying, “I’m too busy”?
  • Which variable is easiest to negotiate in your work: time, scope, sequence or ownership?

Transition

Another difficult situation is knowing how to open the conversation before the problem becomes a missed deadline.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Use the four-step response: acknowledge the request, explain the constraint, offer an alternative and confirm the next step.
  2. Rewrite the response in language you would naturally use at work.
  3. Enter it in the eWorkbook.
  4. Be ready to share it aloud or in chat.
Section 6 · Manage Difficult Situations · Teams, one scenario each

10 · Difficult Situation Practice

Practice diagnosing a time-management conflict and writing the exact opening line for the conversation it requires.

14 min
Go to Whiteboard Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Difficult Situation Practice

Instructor flow

  1. Assign scenarios A through D across groups so each is represented.
  2. Give about 7 minutes in breakout rooms.
  3. Visit rooms and confirm every group has written an actual opening line.
  4. During debrief, ask one group from each scenario to read its line.

Watch for

  • Opening lines that apologize before stating anything.
  • Lines that name the conflict but ask for nothing.

Debrief / listen for

  • Is the opening line clear?
  • Is it professional?
  • Does it make the tradeoff visible?
  • What decision or option does it ask for?

Transition

You have practiced the individual tools. Now you will bring the course together in the capstone.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Your group will receive one scenario.
  2. Name the real time-management issue.
  3. Decide what conversation needs to happen.
  4. Write the exact opening line you would use.
  5. The opening line is the deliverable. Be prepared to read it aloud.
Capstone · Same teams across all steps

11 · Capstone: The Day Changed Again

Bring prioritization, scheduling, buffer and communication together in one changing workday.

35 min
Go to Whiteboard Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Capstone Activity Packet

Instructor flow

  1. Capstone Scenario: give participants time to read the full scenario. Do not let teams begin scheduling yet.
  2. Sort the Work: give teams about 6 minutes to sort the work before scheduling.
  3. Build the Day: reopen the same rooms for about 6 minutes to build the day and leave visible buffer.
  4. Respond to the New Request: give teams about 3 minutes to prepare the 30-second response, then take role-plays in the main room.
  5. Capstone Debrief: ask different teams what they protected, moved, communicated and what reduced the pressure.

Watch for

  • Teams that classify items as urgent only because someone wants them quickly.
  • Schedules that fill every available minute.
  • Teams that make a schedule change but tell nobody affected by it.
  • Role-play responses longer than 30 seconds.

Debrief / listen for

  • What did you protect?
  • What did you move?
  • What did you communicate?
  • What helped reduce the pressure?

Transition

The capstone shows how the tools depend on one another. Now turn them into your own action plan.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Read the full scenario before scheduling anything.
  2. Step 1: Sort the work into what must happen today, what can be scheduled, what needs clarification, and what can be delegated, coordinated or removed.
  3. Step 2: Build the day using fixed commitments first, then protected priority work, then buffer, then whatever routine work fits.
  4. Step 3: Prepare a 30-second response to the new request using Clarify, Tradeoff and Confirm.
  5. Keep the same team for all capstone steps.
Action Plan · Individual, main room

12 · Choose Your Three

Convert the day into three specific, observable commitments tied to the participant’s opening challenge.

7 min
Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Personal Action Plan

Instructor flow

  1. Give about 4 minutes of quiet individual work.
  2. Invite two or three volunteers to share one action.
  3. If an action is vague, ask: What would that look like on Thursday?

Watch for

  • Participants who list too many changes or actions with no connection to their opening challenge.

Debrief / listen for

  • Which of your three actions would make the biggest difference on a bad week?

Transition

Before you leave, make sure at least one action gets off the page.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Review the challenge you entered at the beginning of the webinar.
  2. Choose one action to Start.
  3. Choose one habit to Stop or Reduce.
  4. Choose one practice to Continue or Strengthen.
  5. Make each action specific enough that you could tell whether you did it.
Action Plan · Individual, main room

13 · Commit to the First Step

Get one action scheduled in the participant’s own system before the session ends.

4 min
Open Participant eWorkbook Participant section: Commit to the First Step

Instructor flow

  1. Give about 1 minute to choose and schedule the action.
  2. Ask participants to confirm in chat or with a reaction.
  3. If the room is quiet, give another 30 seconds rather than moving on.

Watch for

  • Actions without a specific day and time. Ask for the missing detail.

Debrief / listen for

  • What exactly will you do, and when will you do it?

Transition

You now have a system, a personal action plan and a first step already scheduled.

Preview learner-facing instructions
  1. Choose one action you will take within the next 24 hours.
  2. Schedule it in your calendar, planner, phone or task system.
  3. Record the same action in the eWorkbook as a backup.
  4. When it is scheduled, use a reaction or chat message to confirm completion.
